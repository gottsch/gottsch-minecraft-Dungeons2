# Changelog for Dungeons2 1.20.1

All notable changes to this project will be documented in this file.

The format is based on [Keep a Changelog](https://keepachangelog.com/en/1.0.0/),
and this project adheres to [Semantic Versioning](https://semver.org/spec/v2.0.0.html).

## [Unreleased]

### Added

- Dungeon hallways can now be made wider, so corridors feel roomier to walk through instead of being a single block wide.
- **Corridors are taller, and their ceilings can be arched.** Passage height is now set by the theme rather than fixed at 5 blocks, and instead of the ceiling meeting the walls square it can spring off them on a course of stairs, so a corridor reads as built rather than as a hole cut through stone. The arch turns corners properly, including the outside of a bend, and a passage only one block wide is never arched shut. Where a corridor does pinch to a single block the ceiling drops a course, so a squeeze feels like a squeeze instead of a tall narrow slot. The classic dungeon's corridors are now 7 blocks high with arched ceilings.
- **Corridor walls can now carry trim, the same way room walls do** — a skirting along the base, a band under the ceiling, or both. Passages were the last plain surface left in the dungeon: every wall in them was bare stonework no matter how decorated the rooms it joined were. Trim is set per corridor style, so a tall vaulted passage and a cramped one can be finished differently, and a band anchored to the ceiling stays put as heights vary. The classic dungeon now runs a polished skirting and an upper band through its vaulted and grand corridors, and a rougher cobble course through the cramped ones. Bear in mind a corridor and the room beside it share a wall, so about half of what you see in a passage is still the room's styling.
- **Corridors change character from floor to floor.** A theme can name several corridor *styles* — a tall vaulted passage, a low cramped one — and each floor of a dungeon rolls one, so descending actually feels like going somewhere rather than walking the same corridor three times. Every corridor on a floor matches, so the passage you're standing in never changes shape halfway along. The classic dungeon now varies between a 7-block vaulted corridor, a grander 8-block one, and a cramped 5-block flat one.
- Dungeon rooms can now be hand-built prefab structures instead of always being generated procedurally, the same way staircases between floors already work. Rooms and staircases can also be organized by theme (e.g. desert-style rooms), so new themed content can be added without any code changes.
- Weathering (moss, cracks, crumbling stone) now ages **stairs, slabs and walls** as well as plain blocks, keeping the block's facing and shape intact instead of resetting it. Ageing can also happen in stages, so a block can decay progressively rather than in one jump.
- Dungeons now grow **cobwebs in corners and creeping lichen on their walls**. The lichen spreads in patches — a patch is more likely to grow next to lichen that's already there, and keeps to a single species — rather than being sprinkled evenly over every wall. Both are datapack-controlled alongside the rest of a theme's weathering.
- Where the stonework has decayed all the way down to dirt, **mushrooms and moss now sprout on top of it and roots hang down underneath**, so a crumbled patch looks overgrown rather than just discoloured.
- **Ledges and corbels now crumble away** when the wall they jutted out of has decayed out from under them, instead of being left hanging in mid-air. One only goes if there is genuinely nothing left touching it.
- **Seagrass and lily pads** on standing water — unused by the classic dungeon, but available to themes that have water.
- **Room floors can now be decorated instead of always being plain**, with five patterns to draw from: a picture-frame **border** ring inset from the edge, a **checkerboard**, a random **speckle** of an accent block, a **cross** through the room's centre, and **spokes** radiating out from it like a compass rose. How often each shows up, its shape (ring inset, cross thickness, spoke count, speckle rarity) and every block it's built from are datapack-controlled per theme. Patterns can also be **layered** — a border ring over a checkerboard floor, say — by listing them together.
- **Room walls can now carry trim** — a stone skirting along the base, a crown moulding under the ceiling, a band at any height in between, or all of them together. Trim can sit flush in the wall or **stand out from it**, so a cornice of upside-down stairs (or a dedicated moulding block) projects into the room the way real trim does, correctly turned on all four walls and never blocking a doorway. A moulding can be positioned relative to the **ceiling** so it stays put as room heights vary. Themes that combine wall trim with a matching floor border get a room that reads as one design rather than two.
- **Ceilings can now be decorated too** — a coffered lattice dividing the ceiling into recessed panels, a band following the walls like a soffit, a decorative boss at the centre, or any combination of them layered together. Themes list ceiling treatments in the order they should be drawn, so an accent lands on top of the pattern beneath it. **Ceiling treatments can hang below the ceiling** rather than sitting flush in it, which is what turns a coffered lattice from a pattern drawn on a flat ceiling into real ribs with the panels recessed between them. Where a room's walls also carry a cornice that stands out into the room, the ribs run into it and interrupt it, the way coffering meets a cornice in a real ceiling — so a room can have both.
- **Not every doorway is hung with a door any more.** Themes set how often an opening gets a real door; the rest stand open. A dungeon where every single door still works reads as a building someone maintains, which is not the one being generated. Open doorways keep their sill and lintel, so they read as framed openings rather than as holes in a wall. The shipped classic dungeon leaves about a third of its doorways open.
- **A room tall enough for a crown moulding now usually has one.** Roughly two thirds of rooms over 5 blocks high carry trim along the top of the wall, instead of about a quarter — trim now reads as how the dungeon is built rather than as a rare find. Floor patterns and ceiling decoration were not sacrificed for it: the shipped theme pairs them *with* the trim (a bordered floor under a crowned wall, a boss on the ceiling above one) rather than competing against it.
- **Rooms can now contain breakable loot pots.** A theme decides how many a room gets, which pot shapes are used, and what they hold — including genuinely rare finds; the shipped classic dungeon puts a small chance of a diamond, emerald or enchanted book behind the everyday bones and string. Pots stand against the walls and stay clear of doorways, and each one's contents are decided when the dungeon generates rather than when you break it.
- The surface entrance is now built from three separate, independently swappable pieces (the building above ground, the shaft down, and the room below) instead of one fixed pair, so new entrance variants can be added later without replacing the whole thing.
- *Development tool:* dungeon pieces can now be built and inspected without starting the game, including the finishing pass that ages the stonework and mitres the corners. That pass previously ran only in a real world, so anything that went wrong in it — a crash during chunk generation, corners left as notches instead of mitred, a hole weathered through a corridor roof — could only be found by walking the dungeon and noticing. Those cases are now covered by tests.
- *Development tool:* the in-game `/d2-generate <pos>` command now takes optional `[size] [floors] [motif]` arguments instead of always forcing a small single-floor classic dungeon, and builds through the real world-generation path — so what it produces is what the world produces, including the proper built entrance and the weathering pass. It previously drew a simplified stand-in with a plain ladder shaft where the entrance should be, which made it misleading for exactly the problems it was being used to find. The target chunks now need to be loaded first.
- *Development tool:* `./gradlew floorplan` plans a dungeon without starting the game and writes an interactive 2D floor-plan viewer to a single HTML file — every floor, level by level, coloured by block or by which piece placed it, with the cells two pieces both wrote flagged. Useful for seeing what a theme's floor patterns and wall trim actually look like across a whole dungeon before building it in game. See `src/test/java/mod/gottsch/forge/dungeons2/diagnostic/README.md`.

### Changed

- The block-ageing and decoration processors now live in **GottschCore 2.9.0** instead of being Dungeons2's own, so other mods can share them. Nothing changes for datapack authors: the `processor_type` values are still `dungeons2:aging` and `dungeons2:decoration`, and every field is unchanged. Dungeons2 now requires GottschCore 2.9.0 or later.
- Work in progress: rebuilding how dungeons get placed into the world so they generate reliably on newer Minecraft versions.
- Corridor ceilings now use the dungeon's motif ceiling block instead of a black concrete placeholder.
- Hand-built structures — the surface entrance, the staircases down, and prefab rooms — are now weathered to match the dungeon around them. Previously only the procedurally generated rooms and corridors aged, so authored pieces stood out as conspicuously pristine.
- Weathering is now defined in vanilla Minecraft's standard `worldgen/processor_list` datapack format instead of a Dungeons2-specific one, so a single file controls both the generated dungeon and the hand-built pieces inside it. **Datapack authors:** the old `data/dungeons2/substitution/*.json` files no longer do anything and can be deleted; see `data/dungeons2/structures/README.md` for the replacement.
- **Datapack authors: a theme can now be split across as many files as it likes.** A theme is a *folder* — everything under `data/dungeons2/dungeons2/motif_config/<theme>/` is read and combined, so the shipped classic theme is now a `base.json` of materials beside `schemes_floors.json`, `schemes_walls.json`, `schemes_ceilings.json` and `schemes_props.json` instead of one 390-line file. A single `motif_config/<theme>.json` still works for a small theme and acts as the base layer if both exist. Files are combined in name order: a section (`wall`, `door`, ...) is taken whole from the last file that writes one, and scheme lists are appended — except that a scheme reusing an earlier scheme's `name` replaces it, which is how an add-on can retune a shipped scheme's weight without restating the list.
- **Datapack authors:** ceiling patterns now take `projection` (default 0), the same knob wall courses have — `1` hangs the treatment a block below the ceiling instead of drawing it flush in it. It wants a `minHeight` on its scheme, since it costs a row of headroom.
- **Datapack authors:** a wall course takes `alternate` (`"random"` by default, or `"strict"`) to choose how `alternateBlock` is mixed in. **A mirrored pair of block halves needs `"strict"`** — randomly mixed, the two halves of a wide brick end up adjacent to themselves and stop reading as whole bricks. `strict` also consumes no randomness, so it renders the same for every seed.
- **Datapack authors:** an individual wall *course* can carry size gates too, not just the slot around it — so one scheme can put a plinth on every wall in the dungeon and add a crown only where there's headroom for it. Note this is not the same as a course being clipped for falling off the wall: a top course in a short room lands on the lintel row and draws, it just looks cramped.
- **Datapack authors: any element slot can now carry its own size gates.** Put `minHeight`/`minSize`/`maxHeight`/`maxSize` inside a `floor`, `wall`, `ceiling` or `pots` slot and that slot alone is dropped in rooms it doesn't fit, while the rest of the scheme still draws — so a bordered floor that wants a crown moulding only where there's headroom is one scheme instead of two. Unlike a gate on the scheme, an element gate changes no probabilities: the scheme still fires at its full weight. The shipped classic theme lost five duplicated schemes to this. **Watch out for gating every slot of a scheme at once** — the room then wins a decorated scheme and draws nothing, which looks fine in any per-scheme breakdown and bare in game.
- **Datapack authors:** room schemes take `maxHeight` and `maxSize` alongside the existing minimums, so a style can be confined to small rooms instead of only being pushed up into large ones. Both are inclusive, both default to no limit, and a maximum below its own minimum is a load error rather than a scheme that silently fits nowhere. Note that upper bounds make it possible to leave a band of room sizes matching no scheme at all — those rooms generate undecorated.
- **Datapack authors:** the `door` section takes `probability` (0.0–1.0, default 1.0) — the chance a doorway is hung with an actual door block. Unlike the block fields beside it, it is optional, because a shape knob has a sensible default where a material does not.
- **Datapack authors:** wall trim courses now take `alternateBlock` and `cornerBlock` alongside `block`, the same pair floors have. `alternateBlock` is mixed into the band at random so a course of rough stone doesn't read as a machined stripe, and `cornerBlock` puts a quoin on the room's four corners. Both default to `block`, so existing trim is unchanged.
- **Datapack authors:** a theme's blocks and floor patterns now live together per theme under `data/dungeons2/dungeons2/motif_config/`, replacing both `block_provider/<theme>.json` and `floor_pattern_config/<theme>.json` — the two used to answer the same question ("which block does this theme use here?") in two different formats. Both old folders are no longer read and can be deleted; see `data/dungeons2/structures/README.md` for the new shape. Every section is optional (omit `door` and you get a sensible default), but a section you *do* write must be complete — a half-filled section now reports an error on load instead of quietly substituting a block you didn't ask for.
- **A room's decoration is now chosen as one coordinated set rather than element by element.** A theme lists *room schemes* — named, weighted styles — and each room rolls exactly one, which then supplies the treatment for its floor (and, as they are added, its walls, ceiling and pillars). Rolling each element separately would have produced rooms nobody designed, with wall trim at an offset that ignores the vaulting above it and a formal floor under rough undecorated walls. A scheme can also require a minimum room height or width, so a style that needs headroom is simply never chosen for a room too small to carry it, instead of being squeezed in and looking wrong. **Datapack authors:** the `floor.patterns` list has moved out of the `floor` section and become a top-level `schemes` list; each old entry becomes a scheme with that entry in its `floor` slot, keeping its weight, plus a name. See `data/dungeons2/structures/README.md`.
- Some theme settings that had never actually been wired up were dropped rather than carried over: the floor's `corner`/`center` blocks, the wall's `corner`/`top_corner`, and the whole `floor_border_pattern` and `floor_drainage_pattern` sections. Nothing read them, so nothing changes visually. Corridor floors, on the other hand, *were* being ignored by mistake and now render the cobblestone/gravel the theme asks for instead of plain stone brick.

### Fixed

- **An arch no longer changes shape where it crosses a chunk boundary.** A corridor's arched ceiling is built in pieces as the world generates, and where one of those pieces straddled a boundary the stonework on the far side hadn't been built yet — so a few corner blocks worked out their shape from empty space and came out square instead of mitred, leaving a faint seam running dead straight through the passage. The arch's corners are now decided from the corridor's own layout, which doesn't depend on what happens to have generated yet.
- **Arched corridors no longer stack a stair in front of a stair.** Where a corner of wall juts into a passage, the arch adds a small capping stair to close off the notch beside it. It was being added even when the arch already reached that spot from the same direction, leaving two stairs in a line one behind the other instead of a clean chamfer — about 86% of those caps were duplicates. Caps that meet the arch at right angles, which is what actually closes a corner, are unchanged.
- **A room's wall decoration is no longer erased by the corridor next to it.** Rooms and the corridors beside them share a wall, and the corridor was being built afterwards — so it painted plain stonework over whatever the room had put there. Roughly half of every room's walls were affected, and no room in a typical dungeon kept all four of its own. Rooms now keep their own walls; a corridor still builds any wall the room isn't already responsible for. Rooms in existing worlds that have already generated are unchanged.
- **The same world seed now always generates the same dungeon.** Roughly one game session in sixteen, a dungeon came out with a different layout than the seed should have produced — a different number of rooms, corridors and doors. The maze picks which direction to dig next from a list, and the order of that list was being decided by the Java runtime rather than by the seed, so it could differ from one launch to the next. **This does change existing dungeons:** a seed generates a slightly different maze than it did before the fix, so dungeons that have not been visited yet in an existing world may come out differently.
- **Staircases built from several stacked pieces now generate.** Previously they never appeared at all: the game reserved space for a staircase before knowing how big the chosen one actually was, and a multi-piece staircase is both larger than the space set aside and offset from it, so it was always rejected — and when it did slip through, it aborted the whole dungeon. The game now builds the staircase once to measure it, sets aside a space that genuinely fits, and then builds it there for real. **Datapack authors:** every element in a staircase pool must use `"projection": "rigid"` for this; see `data/dungeons2/structures/README.md`.
- **A staircase's own built doors are now always connected to.** A door built into a hand-made piece only opened if a corridor happened to arrive at it by chance — nothing routed one there. When that gamble lost, the staircase was sealed off and the game punched a rough hole through its wall somewhere else instead, leaving a finished double door with nothing behind it. Measured across 300 dungeons, this hit **19% of staircase ends**; corridors are now routed to the authored door itself, at both the top and the bottom of the staircase.
- Staircases and prefab rooms are also kept clear of the dungeon floor's outer edge. A door flush against that edge has nothing on its far side, so it could never open at all.
- Staircases between floors no longer sometimes generate as an empty shaft, for the same reason — the space set aside now always matches the staircase that goes in it.
- **Hand-built prefab rooms now appear roughly twice as often.** They are placed the same way as staircases, and had the same problem: the game rotates a prefab when it places it, which moves it off the spot the dungeon had set aside, and 44% of prefab rooms were quietly discarded and replaced with a generated room.
- **A prefab room is no longer built where the dungeon layout rejected it.** The same fault already fixed for staircases: the room got built into the world while the maze had reserved nothing for it, so corridors and other rooms were carved straight through the finished room.
- Wide doorways built into a hand-made structure now open fully. A double door had one half walled up, because the maze deliberately avoids placing two doors side by side and was applying that to the two halves of a single wide doorway.
- A hand-built staircase is no longer placed when the dungeon layout rejected it. The staircase could be built into the world while the maze had reserved nothing for it, so corridors and rooms were carved straight through the finished structure — doors walled over, and the staircase connected to nothing.
- Doorways authored into a multi-piece staircase now actually open. A door's position was judged against the combined footprint of the whole assembled staircase rather than the piece it was built into, so wide doors near the ends were rejected and walled up — a staircase could end up sealed at the top and unconnected at the bottom.
- Corridors no longer build over a prefab's own built-in doorway. A hand-built piece that already contains a real door had a corridor wall laid across it, sealing it shut.
- Creeping lichen no longer appears plastered across doors. The doorway was still a solid wall when the lichen grew, so the lichen latched onto it and stayed there once the door was cut in; rooms and corridors now leave the doorway open at door height from the start.
- Corridors no longer render cutting straight through the middle of nearby rooms. The corridor and room geometry was being placed at the wrong position along one axis; both now line up correctly.
- Fixed a crash that could occur when placing a dungeon with certain room layouts.
- Fixed a dependency version mismatch that could crash the game on startup when Dungeon Blocks was also installed.
- Fixed a misleading warning in the logs about staircase/ladder piece heights that could appear even when everything was working correctly.
